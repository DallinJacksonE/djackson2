## Making it Real

I was in BYU CS 260, which was an intro to Web Development course. We were given the assignment to build a simple website that would meet the requirements of the course. These included custom api calls to the server, having persistent storage for user data, having a websocket connection between the server and client, and building the website eventually in React. We started the course learning about the vanilla HTML, CSS, and Javascript. It was a lot to cover, but I wanted to really feel that when the class ended, I would be able to make a real website that could be used by someone. I thought of my sister.

My sister is quite the entrepreneur. When she was younger she started to crochet baby yoda dolls. These were really popular gifts and she did a good job of them. My Dad encouraged her to consider selling them if it was worth her time. She decided to try it out, and that's how Kaylie's Creations was born. She was selling at local markets and through an instagram page. People were buying a lot of things from her constantly. What ended up becoming a problem was that there was a lot of people messaging her on instagram about buying these plushies but they didn't know what they wanted. Some would ask for specific custom orders that she would have to track, while others would be indesicive. People really needed to be able to just look at her stock and order something that she had already made that matched their needs. This would save Kaylie a lot of time, and the customer would be able to get their order shipped out sooner since they were buying from her stock.

I decided to make the project from my class into a real website that she could use. Starting from zero initial knowledge of Javascript, I put my head down and started to figure things out.

## Finished Product

After four months of learning and spending lots of time in the debugger, I was able to give Kaylie her site. This site was fully functioning for both the user and her as the admin. Clients would make an account, be able to add plushies to a cart, order them, see their order status, and chat with Kaylie in the website. Kaylie was able to see all of her clients and their active orders. She could set the order status, keep records of the fulfilled orders, and have the mailing addresses of her clients on easy access. Other functionality included:

- gallery of photos showcasing new plushies to the shop
- working webstore where users can place orders on in stock items
- working integration with Square pay so clients could pay online
- encrypted user data
- order tracking
- admin tools for listing new products, stock, and order fulfillment
- user tools for seeing order fulfillment and order history
- links to her social media and a calendar for the markets she would attend
